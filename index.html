Between 2008 and 2012, the team played a style of football dubbed 'tiki-taka', a systems approach to football founded upon the ideal of team unity and a comprehensive understanding in the geometry of space on a football field.[45]

Tiki-taka has been variously described as "a style of play based on making your way to the back of the net through short passing and movement",[46] a "short passing style in which the ball is worked carefully through various channels",[47] and a "nonsensical phrase that has come to mean short passing, patience and possession above all else".[48] The style involves roaming movement and positional interchange amongst midfielders, moving the ball in intricate patterns,[49] and sharp, one or two-touch passing.[50] Tiki-taka is "both defensive and offensive in equal measure" – the team is always in possession, so doesn't need to switch between defending and attacking.[51] Commentators have contrasted tiki-taka with "Route One physicality"[46] and with the higher-tempo passing of Barcelona and Arsène Wenger's 2007–08 Arsenal side, which employed Cesc Fàbregas as the only channel between defence and attack.[47] Tiki-taka is associated with flair, creativity, and touch,[52] but can also be taken to a "slow, directionless extreme" that sacrifices effectiveness for aesthetics.[48]
